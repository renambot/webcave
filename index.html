WebCAVE

WebCAVE shows one 3D world across all the screens of a room, such as a CAVE or a wall of displays, so that the people standing in it see the scene around them. Everything runs in the web browser: there is nothing to install.

Use the room

Everything starts from the control page.

Open the control page
  1. Choose the room and what to show. At the top of the control page, pick the display configuration (the layout of the room's screens) and the application (what appears on them), and press switch. The screens change within a second.
  2. Turn the screens on. The control page lists one window per screen. On the computer connected to that screen, open its link (open, or copy link to paste it on another computer) and click once inside the window to make it fullscreen. Its dot on the control page turns green when it is showing.
  3. Look around and move. Press simulator on the control page: it shows all the screens at once and a view of the room from above. Walk with the arrow keys, press H to see every key, or plug in a gamepad.

If the control page says no manager, the cluster service is not running on the server: ask the person who installed WebCAVE.

Try it without a room

The simulator also runs on its own, in this browser, with a virtual room on screen. Click one and walk with the arrow keys:

  • Crayoland · the original 1995 CAVE demo: crayon flowers, bees and birdsong (turn the sound on)
  • A 3D model in a CAVE · a helmet floating in the middle of a four-wall room
  • Aquarium · fish swimming all around you
  • A map across a wall of screens · drag any screen and all of them move together
  • Simple shapes · the quickest check that everything works

Other pages

  • Headset viewer · open it on a VR headset to stand inside the virtual room while the cluster runs
  • Control panel · the current application's own settings, for a tablet
For developers and installers

From a source checkout, start the cluster service with npm run manager before opening the control page; the documentation covers installation, configuration files and Docker deployment.

More simulator examples: wall 3×1, head CAVE (three 46-inch panels), population density map, OpenVDB smoke volume and OpenVDB point cloud (these two need the sample files in public/volumes/).

Simulator parameters: config, app, overview=world, stereo, audio=1, debug=1; app options such as model=URL, size, spin (gltf), vdb=URL (vdb), points=URL (points), style, center=lng,lat, zoom, pitch, bearing (maps). Node windows: node, view, manager=ws://host:8765, stereo, anaglyph=bw; h hides the HUD.
